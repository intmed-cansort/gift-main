Study design and participants

We studied the effects of two distinct intervention design features: 1) the level of personalized genetic education and testing decision support: a technology-assisted patient and family member education and communication tool vs. the tool plus direct assistance from a human navigator; and 2) the cost of the genetic test offered to the relatives in a family ($50 vs free). The inferences that were made related to the main objectives are at both the family cluster and individual level.

Our estimates for the testing aim and invitation aim were the average intervention effect of each intervention on genetic testing and invitation to the GIFT platform, respectively. 



Per the protocol paper, 400 enrolled index patients would have a power of 0.80 or higher to detect an odds ratio of 1.5 for each intervention, given an assumed average of 8 relatives and a residual intraclass correlation (ICC) of 0.15. The power calculation did not account for the benefit of the planned use of baseline covariates, which increased efficiency. Compared to our power calculation, the number of relatives per patient in the enrolled sample was substantially larger than expected at 12, which also increased efficiency. The observed residual ICC was higher than we expected (0.41) and this reduced efficiency relative to the original sample size assumptions.
